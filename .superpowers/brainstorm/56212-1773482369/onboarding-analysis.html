Current Onboarding: What's Wrong & Why

A breakdown of UX, visual, content, and technical issues with the existing 3-screen onboarding

1. Generic, Forgettable Content

Problems

  • "Track Your Health" — every health app says this. Zero differentiation.
  • "AI Swastrica" — the name means nothing to a new user. No benefit shown.
  • "Private & Secure" — security is expected, not a selling point. Feels like filler.
  • Subtitles are generic marketing fluff, not emotionally engaging.
  • Nothing is India-specific — your target audience is Indian users.

2. 3D Models Are a Liability, Not an Asset

Problems

  • Placeholder fallback: If .glb fails to load, users see crude SceneKit shapes (spheres + cones = "heart"). Feels broken.
  • Performance: SceneKit + GLTFKit2 on first launch = slow load, battery drain, potential jank on older iPhones.
  • No emotional connection: A rotating 3D box doesn't make someone trust you with their health data.
  • Double rotation bug: SceneKit rotation (8s loop) + SwiftUI rotation3DEffect (4s oscillation) = chaotic competing animations.
  • Binary size: 3 .glb files + GLTFKit2 dependency just for 3 screens the user sees once.

3. Visual Hierarchy Is Flat

Problems

  • Identical layout on all 3 pages: Model → Title → Subtitle. No visual rhythm or progression.
  • Background is static: PremiumBackground orbs are barely visible (4% opacity in light mode). Effectively a blank white screen.
  • No color storytelling: All 3 pages use the same muted palette. Nothing guides the eye or builds excitement.
  • Skip button appears instantly — signals "this isn't worth your time." Skip should appear only after a brief delay or on page 2+.
  • Page dots + Next button are generic iOS — no brand personality.

4. Animation Issues

Problems

  • All .linear timing — feels robotic. Human-feeling motion uses easeOut/spring curves.
  • Hard reset on swipe: onDisappear resets all offsets to 20px — if you swipe back, content pops harshly instead of sliding.
  • No staggered entrance: Model, title, and subtitle all animate at the same time. Feels like a curtain lifting, not a story unfolding.
  • ParticleField exists in SharedGraphics but isn't used on onboarding — wasted atmospheric potential.

5. Missing the Indian Health Context

Problems

  • No mention of family health management — a huge differentiator for Indian users who manage parents'/in-laws' health.
  • No language warmth — no "Namaste" or cultural touchpoint. Feels like a US app clone.
  • No mention of specific features users care about: medication reminders, doctor visit prep, family member tracking.
  • Doesn't address the trust gap — Indian users are cautious about health apps. Show, don't tell security.

What approach should we take?

Click the approach that resonates — I'll flesh it out in the next screen

A

🎯 Benefit-First Storytelling (Recommended)

Keep 3 screens but rewrite them as emotional benefit stories with Lottie/SwiftUI animations instead of 3D models. Each screen shows a real scenario: "Your mom's BP is high — get notified instantly", "Never miss a medication again", "AI that understands Indian health needs." Lightweight, fast, emotionally resonant.

B

✨ Interactive Feature Tour

4-5 screens with mini-interactions. User taps to "add water", sees a mock notification, or watches the AI respond. Each screen is a micro-experience of the app. More engaging but heavier to build. Risk: users just want to get in.

C

🚀 Single Hero Screen + Quick Start

1 impactful screen — beautiful animated hero with a strong value prop, then straight to sign-up. Fastest path to the app. Works best if you have a strong health profile questionnaire that personalizes the experience. Risk: less time to build trust.
